<!-- ABOUT -->

<section id="about">

    <h2 class="section-title">About Bharathapuzha</h2>

    <div class="about">

        <p>
            Bharathapuzha, popularly known as Nila or Perar,
            is one of the major rivers of Kerala. It is the
            second-longest river in Kerala and plays an important
            role in the physical landscape, agriculture,
            settlements and cultural life of the Malabar region.
        </p>

        <br>

        <p>
            The river originates in the Anamalai Hills of the
            Western Ghats in Tamil Nadu. It flows through the
            Palakkad Gap and traverses the districts of Palakkad,
            Thrissur and Malappuram in Kerala before draining
            into the Arabian Sea near Ponnani.
        </p>

        <br>

        <p>
            Bharathapuzha is an important west-flowing river
            system. Its basin supports agricultural activities,
            settlements, irrigation systems and diverse
            ecological environments.
        </p>

    </div>

    <div class="stats">

        <div class="stat-card">
            <h3>209 km</h3>

        <div class="stat-card">

            <p>Source Region</p>
        </div>

<!-- GEOGRAPHICAL FEATURES -->

<section class="features" id="features">

    <h2 class="section-title">
        Geographical Features
    </h2>

    <div class="feature-grid">

        <div class="feature-card">
            <h3>01. Origin and Source</h3>

            <p>
                The river system originates in the Anamalai Hills
                of the Western Ghats. The mountainous source region
                contributes rainfall runoff and streams to the
                upper drainage basin.
            </p>
        </div>

        <div class="feature-card">
            <h3>02. Drainage Basin</h3>

            <p>
                The Bharathapuzha basin extends across Tamil Nadu
                and Kerala. It includes highlands, valleys,
                agricultural plains and coastal environments.
            </p>
        </div>

        <div class="feature-card">
            <h3>03. River Tributaries</h3>

            <p>
                Important tributaries include Kannadipuzha,
                Kalpathipuzha, Gayathripuzha and Thuthapuzha.
                These tributaries contribute water to the
                main river channel.
            </p>
        </div>

        <div class="feature-card">
            <h3>04. River Course</h3>

            <p>
                The river flows westward through the Palakkad Gap.
                Its course includes mountainous terrain,
                middle reaches, floodplains and a coastal
                outlet near Ponnani.
            </p>
        </div>

        <div class="feature-card">
            <h3>05. Seasonal Flow</h3>

            <p>
                River discharge is strongly influenced by the
                southwest and northeast monsoons. Water levels
                generally decrease during the dry summer season.
            </p>
        </div>

        <div class="feature-card">
            <h3>06. Landforms</h3>

            <p>
                The river basin contains valleys, river terraces,
                floodplains, alluvial deposits, channel bars
                and sandbanks formed by fluvial processes.
            </p>
        </div>

    </div>

</section>

<!-- RIVER COURSE -->

<section class="course" id="course">

    <h2 class="section-title">
        River Course and Drainage
    </h2>

    <div class="course-box">

        <h3>Upper Course</h3>

        <p>
            The upper drainage system begins in the Anamalai
            Hills of the Western Ghats. Steep slopes and
            mountainous terrain influence runoff and stream flow.
        </p>

        <br>

        <h3>Middle Course</h3>

        <p>
            The river enters Kerala through the Palakkad Gap.
            It receives water from major tributaries and flows
            through Palakkad and parts of Thrissur.
            River valleys, agricultural landscapes and
            settlements are prominent in this region.
        </p>

        <br>

        <h3>Lower Course</h3>

        <p>
            The river flows through Malappuram district.
            The channel becomes associated with wider
            floodplain environments and finally reaches
            the Arabian Sea near Ponnani.
        </p>

        <br>

        <h3>Major Tributaries</h3>

        <ul>
            <li>Kannadipuzha (Chitturpuzha)</li>
            <li>Kalpathipuzha</li>
            <li>Gayathripuzha</li>
            <li>Thuthapuzha (Thoothapuzha)</li>
        </ul>

    </div>

</section>

<!-- CULTURAL HERITAGE -->

<section class="features">

    <h2 class="section-title">
        Cultural Heritage of Nila
    </h2>

    <div class="about">

        <p>
            Bharathapuzha is widely regarded as a cultural
            river of Kerala. Its banks have influenced
            Malayalam literature, traditional art forms
            and religious practices.
        </p>

        <br>

        <p>
            Thirunavaya, an important historical and
            cultural centre, is located along the river.
            The Mamankam festival was historically
            associated with this region.
        </p>

        <br>

        <p>
            Kerala Kalamandalam, a renowned institution
            for traditional performing arts, is situated
            at Cheruthuruthy near Bharathapuzha.
            The river has inspired many Malayalam
            writers, poets and artists.
        </p>

    </div>

</section>

<!-- ENVIRONMENTAL CONSERVATION -->

<section class="conservation">

    <h2 class="section-title">
        Environmental Challenges and Conservation
    </h2>

    <div class="about">

        <p>
            Bharathapuzha faces several environmental
            challenges that affect its water resources
            and river ecosystem.
        </p>

        <br>

        <ul>

            <li>
                Excessive sand mining and alteration
                of the river channel.
            </li>

            <li>
                Water scarcity during the summer season.
            </li>

            <li>
                Pollution from domestic waste
                and other human activities.
            </li>

            <li>
                Changes in river flow due to dams
                and water abstraction.
            </li>

            <li>
                Degradation of riparian vegetation
                and aquatic habitats.
            </li>

        </ul>

        <br>

        <p>
            Sustainable river management, restoration
            of riparian vegetation, control of pollution,
            scientific sand mining regulation and
            community participation are important
            for the conservation of Bharathapuzha.
        </p>

    </div>

</section>
